# Discovery context for planning

Use this while clarifying a new project and when handing that work to the PRD executor. A short brief is a navigation aid; the full requirements context is the input to planning.

## Preserve the conversation's meaning

Keep one discovery record beside the external [handoff](sessions.md), outside task worktrees. Reuse an existing durable record when suitable. Link it from the active handoff with an absolute path. The coordinator is its sole writer; research agents return reports for the coordinator to save alongside it. This record is local working context, not a new required public project document.

Start with the original request and supplied materials. After each interview answer, preserve:

- The question and the user's full relevant answer, including examples, qualifications, constraints and preferences. Keep exact wording where meaning depends on it; exclude unrelated conversation and secrets.
- The resulting decision and why it was made, using stable references such as D01. Separate explicit user decisions, delegated choices, agent assumptions, research observations and open questions.
- Alternatives rejected or deferred and their reasons; later corrections with the earlier decision marked superseded. Keep the current decision clear without erasing how it changed.
- Coverage of the requirement tree, including open branches, their dependencies, nonblocking uncertainties and the exact next question if interrupted.

Maintain a current overview of the user/problem, journeys, full agreed scope, priorities, exclusions, constraints and observable success. Preserve the detailed Q&A beneath it or in linked durable attachments; compression and handoff must retain that detail. Record the final design selection and its provenance here too.

Attach both research reports with sources and dates/revisions, concrete implications and limitations. Record each material suggestion as accepted within existing scope, explicitly accepted by the user, rejected, deferred or still open. Research suggestions do not become requirements merely by appearing in a report. Retain technical reuse findings for the later stack and implementation sessions.

## Deliver the full package to a fresh PRD session

Before transfer, reconcile the overview against every answer and correction. Add a reading manifest to the handoff covering the discovery record, any Q&A attachments, supplied requirement/reference material, both research reports, and the design selection. Give real paths, purpose and reading order. Include a checklist of the decisions and constraints the PRD must cover; do not repeat the full record in the launch prompt.

Use the normal immutable handoff snapshot to identify the exact discovery revision and attachments being transferred. Verify that the successor can read every required file; preserve necessary content from ephemeral chat, images or temporary downloads in durable, accessible form. Retain source links as provenance, not as a substitute for unavailable essential content. Keep private execution paths in the handoff rather than public project documents.

The PRD successor reads the entire discovery record and its relevant Q&A attachments, both research reports and supplied requirement material before drafting. Read long files in sections instead of silently truncating them or relying only on the overview. An unavailable input is a context gap: recover it from the predecessor's saved material or identify the exact missing information before drafting affected requirements. Inherit settled decisions; ask only about contradictions or material gaps that the saved sources cannot resolve.

Before handing on the PRD, reconcile every current decision in the manifest with a PRD section, requirement or explicit exclusion/deferred item. Record this coverage in the discovery/handoff context, with unresolved items and reasons. A summary that drops a requirement, qualification or rejected alternative does not satisfy this check. Later design and technical sessions use the same package as needed; task context points developers to applicable reuse findings.
